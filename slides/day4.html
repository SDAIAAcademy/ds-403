A grader, a small set, and an interval

A grader's Arabic specificity is 0.73, from 49 human-failed answers. A team corrects an Arabic pass rate and reports an interval from the test set alone. What should the reviewer ask for?

Nothing: the corrected rate is unbiased
A Clopper-Pearson interval on the test set
An interval that also resamples the calibration verdicts
The uncorrected rate, which has a narrower interval
Why. Sensitivity and specificity are estimates from a small labelled set; their uncertainty belongs in the corrected rate's interval.

Answer: C. Option D is the tempting retreat: narrower, and biased.

Contamination

Test questions inside the tuning examples inflate the score on exactly those questions

  • Pre-training contamination: a public benchmark in a model's training data.
  • Tuning contamination: test items among the few-shot or prompt-tuning examples.
  • The model then recalls answers instead of reading the regulation.

The test

Find the overlap, then compare each configuration's score on overlapping and clean questions.

The contamination survey reviews detection methods; for an in-house set the simplest check is exact and normalised matching against everything the configuration saw. AE-303 checked a golden set for overlap with prompt examples; here we measure what the overlap did to the score.

Sources: arXiv 2406.04244.

One more threat

A saturated or public benchmark cannot separate strong systems

  • When every system scores near the ceiling, differences sit inside the noise (Day 3).
  • A public set may be in some systems' training data and not others'.
  • Keep a private, versioned set, refreshed as regulations change.

The catalog lists benchmark sets and their saturation among the things that must be refreshed. Bunyan's set is private and built from its own regulations, which protects it from pre-training contamination but not from the tuning contamination we just found.

Checkpoint

A fair comparison

A report compares two configurations. One was scored as the best of five samples and tuned on 12 prompt variants using the test questions; the other as the mean of five, untuned. What must the audit do first?

Rescore both the same way and remove the test questions used in tuning before comparing
Add confidence intervals to the reported numbers
Ask for more test questions
Accept it: the tuned configuration earned its lead
Why. Unequal scoring and tuning on the test set are biases, not noise; only an equal-budget comparison on clean questions measures the configurations rather than the procedure.

Answer: A. Intervals come second: on a biased comparison they are precise about the wrong thing.

02
Auditing a claim

Every claim is supported, unsupported or untestable

Supported

The data back it at the strength stated.

Unsupported

The available data contradict it, or do not back it as stated.

Untestable

No data the authority holds could decide it.

From DS-305. The same three classes were used for model-card claims; here the evidence is statistical.

Recap from DS-305 day 1. "Unsupported" is not "false": a claim of 15 points is unsupported even though D is not worse than C. The audit's job is to say what the evidence supports, so the memo always proposes the claim that would be supported.

Untestable claims

An untestable claim tells the decision owner what evidence to commission

C7 · the 2027 regulations

A new population: size a new set (Day 2: 417 questions for three points).

C8 · applicants' preference

Needs a preference study or an online experiment (DS-302), not grader verdicts.

Untestable is not a dismissal. In a memo it comes with the study that would test it and its rough cost; that turns the audit into a plan.

The audit memo

One line per claim: the class, the number, the claim that would be supported

C4 unsupported: 40 of 40 bounds D's unsafe rate below 7.2% at 95%; it does not show "never". Supported version: "D gave no unsafe answer on the 40-question safety set; with 95% confidence its unsafe rate on such questions is below 7.2%."

The memo is the evidence for LO5. It goes to Dr. Nadeen, so it is written for a decision owner: no statistics vocabulary without its number, and every unsupported claim paired with the version the data support.

Auditing colleagues' work

Audit the claims, not the person

  • Khattab's numbers were computed correctly; the comparison was not fair.
  • Show every re-analysis with its code, so the author can rerun it.
  • A standard (session 4) prevents the next report from needing an audit.

The SDAIA AI Ethics Principles ask for transparency and accountability in how AI systems are assessed; an audit that the author can rerun serves both. Keep the room's tone constructive: most of these faults are common practice, not misconduct.

Checkpoint

Classing a claim

A report claims "the assistant is equally accurate for all regions". The logs carry no region field. How should the audit class the claim?

Supported: no regional difference was found
Unsupported: equal accuracy is unlikely
Supported, because regions should not matter for regulations
Untestable with these logs; name the data that would test it
Why. Without a region field no analysis of these logs can decide the claim; the memo says what data would.

Answer: D. If the room is split between B and D, the distinction is: unsupported means the data in hand speak against or fail to back it; untestable means they cannot speak to it at all.

Testing the gap

Test the gap itself, clustered by document, because most documents have questions in both languages

  • Each Arabic question gets weight +1 / nar, each English one −1 / nen.
  • Sum the weighted deviations within each document, then square.
  • A hard document lowers both languages; that part cancels.

Checkpoint

Two overlapping intervals

C's Arabic interval is 63.8 to 74.6 and its English interval 69.3 to 80.8. They overlap. Most documents have questions in both languages. What can you conclude about the gap?

No gap: the intervals overlap
Nothing yet: test the gap itself with a standard error clustered by document
Compute the gap's SE as the root sum of the two squared SEs
Report both rates; gaps are never tested
Why. Two slices that share documents are correlated; only an interval for the gap itself, clustered by document, says whether the gap is real.

Answer: B. The independent-samples SE (option C) is close here, but it is the wrong method whenever slices share clusters.

Fifteen fields in the lab's reference version; this table groups them. A two-page standard: one line per field with its reason and an example on page one, the worked example on page two.

Make it checkable

A validator turns the standard into a gate

def validate(report):
    problems = [f"missing: {f}" for f in STANDARD if not report.get(f)]
    if not {"ar", "en"} <= set(report.get("slices") or {}):
        problems.append("slices: Arabic and English required")
    if report.get("shared_items") and "paired" not in report.get("comparison", ""):
        problems.append("comparison must be paired")
    return problems

The lab's version also rejects naive or Wald intervals and a grader without per-language sensitivity and specificity. The stretch turns it into a release gate that raises an error: in the capstone it runs in the repository's tests.
