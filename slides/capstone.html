Two or more open-weight configurations, each measured. A claim without an interval is a plan, not a result.

Full marks, criterion by criterion. Plan: plan.json committed before the first log, with the metric, the minimum effect, the clustering unit and the test, amendments dated. Uncertainty and comparison: clustered intervals with design effects, a paired difference against the minimum effect, and an adjusted family when there are more than two configurations. Sizing: formula and simulation agree, and the README says what the next evaluation should change. Grader correction and slicing: sensitivity and specificity per language from the participant's own labels, corrected scores with intervals that carry the calibration's uncertainty. Reporting and reproducibility: the README fills the standard's fields and one command rebuilds every number.

The rubric is 60% of the grade; the supervised check and the review are 20% each. Passing needs 70% overall and at least 60% on the rubric and on the check.

Format

Fifteen minutes each: ten to present, five for the challenge

1 · The plan

The claim, the minimum effect, the clustering unit; the plan.json commit and its date.

2 · The results

Scores with clustered intervals, the paired verdict, Arabic and English after the grader correction.

3 · One choice

One statistical choice you made, and what the other choice would have cost.

Peer feedback. After each review, everyone writes one strength, one question and one suggestion.

Keep time strictly: ten minutes, then the challenge. No live model calls during the talk: the logs are the evidence, and re-running the analysis takes seconds. A participant who used a task set of their own instead of Bunyan's shows its licence and its cluster column first.

What earns the marks. A prediction with a reason, then an honest reading of the re-run, whichever way it went.

Pick the change from the repository: drop the document with the most questions, or the one where the configurations differ most; or move the minimum effect just above the lower bound of the paired interval, so the verdict should move from "worth the switch" to "better, not shown to matter". Score the reasoning (fewer clusters, a wider interval; a stricter threshold, the same interval, another verdict), not whether the guess was exact. This is the 20% live review.
